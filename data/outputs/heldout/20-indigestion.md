# **Understanding Indigestion**

Almost everyone has indigestion at some point. Indigestion is discomfort or a burning feeling in the upper part of your stomach.

### **What Are the Symptoms of Indigestion?**

You may have one or more of these symptoms:

- Heartburn (a burning feeling in your chest or throat).
- Burping.
- Bloating (feeling full or swollen in your stomach).
- Nausea (feeling sick to your stomach).
- Vomiting (throwing up).
- Pain or discomfort in your upper stomach.

### **What Causes Indigestion?**

Several things can cause indigestion or make it worse, including:

- Eating too much food.
- Eating too quickly.
- Eating foods that are high in fat.
- Eating when you feel stressed.
- Smoking.
- Drinking too much alcohol.
- Taking certain medicines.
- Being tired.
- Having ongoing stress.

Sometimes, indigestion is caused by a health problem, such as:

- **Stomach ulcers:** Sores in the lining of your stomach.
- **GERD (acid reflux):** A condition where stomach acid flows back into your throat.

### **How Can You Prevent or Relieve Indigestion?**

You may feel better if you:

- Avoid foods that cause indigestion.
- Eat slowly.
- Avoid eating too much at one time.
- Try to reduce stress.
- Avoid smoking and drinking too much alcohol.

### **When Should You See a Health Care Provider?**

Indigestion can sometimes be a sign of a more serious health problem.

Contact your health care provider if:

- Your indigestion lasts longer than two weeks.
- You have severe stomach pain.
- You have other symptoms that concern you.

### **How Is Indigestion Diagnosed and Treated?**

Your health care provider may use different tests to find out what is causing your indigestion, such as:

- **X-rays:** Pictures that help doctors look inside your body.
- **Lab tests:** Tests that check samples of your blood or other body fluids.
- **Upper endoscopy:** A test that uses a thin tube with a small camera to look inside your upper digestive system.

Your provider may also prescribe medicine to help relieve your symptoms or treat the cause.

### **Remember**

Indigestion is common and often improves when you avoid foods and activities that trigger it. If it lasts more than two weeks or causes severe pain, talk with your health care provider.
