# **How Much Calcium Do You Need Each Day?**

Calcium is an important mineral that helps keep your bones and teeth strong. The amount of calcium you need each day depends on your age and whether you are pregnant or breastfeeding.

The recommended daily amounts are listed below in milligrams (mg).

### **How Much Calcium Do You Need?**

| Age or group                     | Amount needed each day |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Babies from birth to 6 months    | 200 mg                 |
| Babies 7–12 months               | 260 mg                 |
| Children 1–3 years               | 700 mg                 |
| Children 4–8 years               | 1,000 mg               |
| Children 9–13 years              | 1,300 mg               |
| Teens 14–18 years                | 1,300 mg               |
| Adults 19–50 years               | 1,000 mg               |
| Men 51–70 years                  | 1,000 mg               |
| Women 51–70 years                | 1,200 mg               |
| Adults 71 years and older        | 1,200 mg               |
| Pregnant or breastfeeding teens  | 1,300 mg               |
| Pregnant or breastfeeding adults | 1,000 mg               |

### **Who May Have Trouble Getting Enough Calcium?**

Some people may be more likely to not get enough calcium, including:

- Children and teens ages 4–18.
- People who are Black or Asian.
- Adults age 50 and older who have a low income.
- Women who have gone through menopause. After menopause, the body has a harder time taking in and keeping calcium.
- People who do not drink milk or eat other dairy foods.

### **Should You Take Calcium Supplements?**

Calcium supplements are pills or other products that help you get more calcium.

Talk with your health care provider before taking calcium supplements. Your provider can help you decide if you need them and how much to take.

Your provider may also recommend a blood test to check your calcium levels.

**Remember:** Getting enough calcium helps keep your bones healthy. Eating calcium-rich foods and talking with your health care provider can help you meet your daily needs.

*Source: NIH – National Institutes of Health, Office of Dietary Supplements*
