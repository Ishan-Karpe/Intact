# **Understanding Cholesterol Tests for Children and Teens**

A cholesterol blood test measures the amount of cholesterol and other fats in your blood. It helps your health care provider find out if your cholesterol levels are healthy.

The test measures five things:

### **What Does the Cholesterol Test Measure?**

- **Total cholesterol:** The total amount of cholesterol in your blood. This includes both good and bad cholesterol.
- **LDL (bad cholesterol):** Too much LDL can build up inside your blood vessels and cause blockages.
- **HDL (good cholesterol):** Helps remove extra cholesterol from your blood vessels.
- **Non-HDL cholesterol:** Includes LDL and other types of cholesterol. It is your total cholesterol minus your HDL.
- **Triglycerides:** Another type of fat in your blood. High levels can increase your risk of heart disease.

### **Healthy Cholesterol Levels for Children and Teens (Age 19 and Younger)**

| Type of cholesterol    | Healthy level       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------- |
| Total cholesterol      | Less than 170 mg/dL |
| Non-HDL cholesterol    | Less than 120 mg/dL |
| LDL (bad cholesterol)  | Less than 110 mg/dL |
| HDL (good cholesterol) | More than 45 mg/dL  |

Cholesterol levels are measured in milligrams per deciliter (mg/dL).

### **When Should Children Get a Cholesterol Test?**

The right time for your child to have a cholesterol test depends on their age, health, risk factors, and family history.

General recommendations include:

- **First test:** Between ages 9 and 11.
- **Repeat tests:** Every 5 years after the first test.
- **Earlier testing:** Some children may need a cholesterol test as early as age 2 if their family has a history of high cholesterol, heart attacks, or strokes.

### **Remember**

Children can have high cholesterol even if they seem healthy. A blood test can help find problems early.

Talk with your child's health care provider about when your child should have a cholesterol test and how often it should be repeated.
