# **How Is Glaucoma Found?**

A complete eye exam can help your eye doctor find out if you have glaucoma. Glaucoma is an eye disease that can damage the nerve that connects your eye to your brain. It can cause vision loss if it is not treated.

A complete eye exam may include these tests:

### **1. Dilated Eye Exam**

Your eye doctor will put special drops in your eyes to make your pupils (the black circles in the middle of your eyes) bigger.

This allows more light into your eyes so your doctor can get a better look at the back of your eyes. They use a special magnifying lens to check for glaucoma and other eye problems.

After the exam:

- Your vision may be blurry for a few hours.
- Your eyes may be more sensitive to light.
- You may need someone to drive you home.

### **2. Visual Field Test**

This test checks your side vision (what you can see to the sides without moving your eyes).

It helps your eye doctor find out if glaucoma has affected your vision.

### **3. Tonometry (Eye Pressure Test)**

This test measures the pressure inside your eyes. High pressure can increase your risk of glaucoma.

### **Who Is at Higher Risk for Glaucoma?**

Some people have a higher chance of getting glaucoma. You may be at higher risk if you:

- Are Black or African American and over age 40.
- Are over age 60, especially if you are Hispanic or Latino.
- Have a family member who has glaucoma.
- Have high blood pressure.
- Have diabetes.

### **How Often Should You Get Your Eyes Checked?**

If you are at higher risk for glaucoma, you should have a complete eye exam every 2 to 4 years.

If you have high blood pressure or diabetes, you should have a dilated eye exam at most once a year.

Talk with your eye doctor about your risk of glaucoma and how often you should have your eyes checked.

### **Remember**

Glaucoma may cause vision loss if it is not found and treated early. Regular eye exams can help find the disease before serious damage happens.
