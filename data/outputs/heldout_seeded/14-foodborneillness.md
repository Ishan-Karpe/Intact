# **Understanding Foodborne Illness (Food Poisoning)**

Every year, about 48 million people in the United States get sick from eating food that has harmful germs or other unsafe substances in it.

Food can become contaminated by:

- **Bacteria:** Tiny germs that can make you sick.
- **Viruses:** Germs that can cause illness.
- **Parasites:** Tiny living organisms that can cause infections.
- **Harmful chemicals:** Substances such as high amounts of pesticides.

### **What Are the Symptoms?**

Symptoms depend on what caused the illness. They can be mild or serious.

Common symptoms include:

- Upset stomach.
- Stomach cramps or pain.
- Nausea (feeling sick to your stomach).
- Vomiting (throwing up).
- Diarrhea (loose or watery stools).
- Fever.
- Dehydration (losing too much water from your body).

Most food-related illnesses start suddenly and last a short time. This is called an **acute illness**.

### **How Does Food Become Contaminated?**

Food goes through many steps before it reaches your table. Germs and harmful substances can get into food at any of these steps.

For example:

- Raw meat can become contaminated during the process of preparing it for sale.
- Fruits and vegetables can become contaminated while growing or during food processing.
- Cold foods can become unsafe if they are left out in warm temperatures for too long.
- Food can become contaminated in your kitchen if it is left at room temperature for more than 2 hours.

### **How Can You Prevent Foodborne Illness?**

Safe food handling can help prevent food poisoning.

Some ways to keep food safe include:

- Wash your hands before preparing or eating food.
- Wash fruits and vegetables.
- Keep raw meat separate from other foods.
- Cook food to a safe temperature.
- Keep cold foods refrigerated.
- Do not leave food at room temperature for more than 2 hours.

### **How Is Foodborne Illness Treated?**

Most people get better on their own without special treatment.

To help your body recover:

- Drink plenty of fluids to replace the water you lose.
- Replace lost electrolytes (minerals that help your body work properly).
- Get enough rest.

If your health care provider finds the cause of your illness, they may prescribe medicine, such as antibiotics, if needed.

If the illness is serious, you may need treatment in a hospital.

### **Remember**

Foodborne illness can often be prevented by handling, cooking, and storing food safely. If you become sick, replacing lost fluids is important to prevent dehydration.

*Source: NIH – National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases*
