# How Much Sleep Do You Need?

The amount of sleep you need depends on your age, daily activities, health, and sleep habits. General sleep recommendations are:

- **Babies (4–12 months):** 12–16 hours a day, including naps
- **Children (1–2 years):** 11–14 hours a day, including naps
- **Children (3–5 years):** 10–13 hours a day, including naps
- **Children (6–12 years):** 9–12 hours a day
- **Teenagers (13–18 years):** 8–10 hours a day
- **Adults (18 years and older):** 7–9 hours a day

## Sleep and Age

During puberty, a teenager's internal body clock changes. This makes them feel sleepy later at night and want to wake up later in the morning. This can be difficult when school starts early, which is one reason many teenagers do not get enough sleep.

Some people believe adults need less sleep as they get older, but research does not support this. However, older adults often sleep less, spend less time in deep sleep, and wake up more easily during the night.

## Why Sleep Quality Matters

Getting enough hours of sleep is important, but how well you sleep matters too. If you wake up often during the night, you may not get enough time in the deeper stages of sleep that help your body rest.

To find out if you are getting enough good-quality sleep, ask yourself:

- Do I have trouble waking up in the morning?
- Is it hard for me to focus during the day?
- Do I fall asleep or feel sleepy during the day?

If you answered yes to any of these questions, you may need to improve your sleep habits.
