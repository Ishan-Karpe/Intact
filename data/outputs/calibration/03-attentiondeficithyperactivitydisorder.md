# How Is ADHD Diagnosed?

There is no single test that can tell whether someone has ADHD. Instead, doctors use several steps to decide if a person has ADHD.

First, the person usually has a physical exam. This may include vision and hearing tests for both children and adults. These tests help rule out other health problems that could cause similar symptoms.

Other conditions can also cause symptoms that look like ADHD, such as anxiety, depression, sleep problems, and certain learning disabilities.

Doctors also ask about the person's medical history and family history. They usually use ADHD checklists, questionnaires, or interviews to learn more about the person's symptoms. These tools use scoring systems to help doctors decide whether the person's symptoms and history match ADHD.

## What Is Needed for an ADHD Diagnosis?

To diagnose ADHD, a doctor must find all of the following:

- **Symptoms before age 12:** Several ADHD symptoms must have started before the person turned 12.
- **Symptoms lasting at least 6 months:** Several symptoms of trouble paying attention and/or hyperactivity and impulsive behavior must have lasted for at least 6 months and cause serious problems.
- **Enough symptoms for the person's age:**
  - Children up to age 16 must have at least 6 ongoing symptoms.
  - People age 17 and older must have at least 5 ongoing symptoms.
- **Symptoms in at least 2 settings:** Symptoms must happen in more than one place, such as at home and at school or work.
- **Problems with daily life:** Symptoms must clearly make it harder to do well at school, work, or in social situations.
- **Not caused by another mental health condition:** The symptoms must not be better explained by another mental health disorder.
