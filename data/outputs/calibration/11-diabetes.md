# Risk Factors for Different Types of Diabetes

Different types of diabetes have different risk factors. A risk factor is something that can increase your chance of developing a health condition.

## Type 1 Diabetes

You can develop type 1 diabetes at any age, but it most often begins during childhood. Having a parent, brother, or sister with type 1 diabetes may increase your risk.

## Type 2 Diabetes

You may have a higher chance of developing type 2 diabetes if you:

- Are overweight or have obesity.
- Are over 35 years old. Children, teenagers, and young adults can also develop type 2 diabetes, but it is more common in middle-aged and older adults.
- Have a family history of diabetes.
- Have prediabetes. This means your blood sugar is higher than normal but not high enough to be diabetes.
- Had diabetes during pregnancy (gestational diabetes).
- Have given birth to a baby weighing 9 pounds or more.
- Are African American, American Indian, Asian American, Hispanic/Latino, or Pacific Islander.
- Do not get enough physical activity.
- Have certain health conditions, such as high blood pressure or polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).

## Gestational Diabetes

Gestational diabetes is diabetes that develops during pregnancy. You may have a higher chance of developing it if you:

- Are overweight or have obesity.
- Have a family history of diabetes.
- Had gestational diabetes during an earlier pregnancy.
- Have given birth to a baby weighing 9 pounds or more.
- Have polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).
- Are African American, Hispanic/Latino, American Indian, Alaska Native, Native Hawaiian, or Pacific Islander.
