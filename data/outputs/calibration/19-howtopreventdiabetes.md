# Risk Factors for Type 2 Diabetes

Many people have a chance of developing type 2 diabetes. Your risk depends on a mix of factors, including your genes, health, and lifestyle.

Some things that can increase your risk include:

- Having **prediabetes**, which means your blood sugar is higher than normal but not high enough to be called diabetes.
- Being overweight or having obesity.
- Being 35 years old or older.
- Having a family history of diabetes.
- Being African American, Alaska Native, American Indian, Asian American, Hispanic/Latino, Native Hawaiian, or Pacific Islander.
- Having high blood pressure.
- Having low levels of HDL (good) cholesterol or high levels of triglycerides (a type of fat in your blood).
- Having had diabetes during pregnancy.
- Having given birth to a baby weighing 9 pounds or more.
- Not getting enough physical activity.
- Having a history of heart disease or stroke.
- Having depression.
- Having polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), a condition that affects hormone levels.
- Having **acanthosis nigricans**, a skin condition that causes dark, thick patches of skin, usually around the neck or armpits.
- Smoking.
